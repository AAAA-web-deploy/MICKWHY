# Website benchmark notes — checked October 12, 2026

These notes reflect page structure and extracted media/links accessible at research time. They are not claims of direct frame-by-frame inspection of animations or exact source CSS.

## 1. https://wormholewassie.lol/
- Homepage starts with a creature-driven premise in one paragraph, with hero art and a copyable contract address.
- Visual "Lore" story reads like Issue #1, with a large comic-cover image and supporting illustrations, then a tap-to-view "The Panels" gallery.
- Utility is integrated into story: How to Buy, Chart placeholder, Join the Ride.
- Asset URLs inspected: `/assets/gen/surf.webp`, `/assets/img/comic.webp`, `/assets/gen/highfive.webp`, and `/assets/img/banner.webp`.
- **Take**: character-led hero, picture-first episodic storytelling, lightbox/gallery metaphor, concise meme voice, short transaction-information portion.
- **Do not take**: the Wassie character, its artwork, its multiverse story, wording or layouts pixel-for-pixel.

## 2. https://trololol.io/
- Homepage is content-sparse: "troll", one-line positioning, social icons, a GIF, buy/memes/merch and market links, visible contract address.
- Site prominently leans on instant recognizability of trollface. No long project explanation is central to extracted content.
- **Take**: instant meme recognition, minimal narrative burden, direct social/call-to-action hierarchy, repeatable emotive face.
- **Do not take**: trollface character, third-party GIF or media, existing branding, claims of licensed original IP, or unrelated links.

## Creative synthesis
A new orange **comedy stage / wrong-duo variety show** design: Mick as MC, huge original imagery, 2 amusing illustrated performances, an actual small React randomizer, emotion portraits, `Performance` community section, and unobtrusive Ethereum identity/trust info. It must not look like a clone of either reference.
